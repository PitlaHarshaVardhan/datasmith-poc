Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is defined as abnormalities of kidney structure or function present for more than three months, with implications for health. Common causes include diabetes, hypertension, and glomerulonephritis.

Edema (swelling), reduced urine output, and shortness of breath in CKD patients may indicate fluid overload or worsening kidney function. Evaluation usually includes monitoring weight, blood pressure, and urine output. [Ref 1]

In patients with acute kidney injury or CKD, persistent vomiting, confusion, and markedly reduced urine output are warning signs that require urgent medical attention. These can suggest electrolyte imbalance, uremia, or severe volume depletion. [Ref 2]

Nephrology guidelines generally recommend that patients with severe symptoms such as chest pain, severe shortness of breath, confusion, or very low urine output seek emergency care immediately rather than waiting for a routine appointment. [Ref 3]

Patients discharged after an episode of acute kidney injury are advised to monitor their blood pressure, hydration status, urine output, and symptoms like vomiting or dizziness. Any sudden worsening or combination of these symptoms should prompt contacting a healthcare provider right away. [Ref 4]
